> UNII Corps log date 2327-09-31-2334 5min earlier

Hector found time to be less linear than usual.

It was the strangest sensation. He felt like the current that had been slowing him down, the weighty drag that
constantly restricted his movements, was not actually restricting him, but rather a restriction by himself. He felt, but
had yet been able to verify, that if he strained hard enough, he would be able to pierce the current that encircled him
and see and move through time. Even more confusingly the restriction 'felt' like it was something he was actively doing.
Hector likened it to being in two places at once, with one version fighting to move, the other pushing back to stop
'himself' from moving too fast. His brain struggled to piece it all together.

On top of it all, the others were constantly interrupting him and prodding him forwards. On one hand he was grateful to
be leaving this place, with its terrible tortured victims. The victims. He routinely found himself a silent and
abhorrent witness to the worst crimes imaginable. People dissected alive, experimented, discarded, and killed. Hopefully
killed. With infinite kaleidoscopic views, Hector had witnessed countless of these atrocities on unending repeat.
Screams of agony, terror, and rage reverberated through his very bones. Humans turned into the medical equivalent of
spare parts, and indeed some were, and left dissembled in vats. Nobody was safe here. Even the experimenters were at
constant risk of becoming experimented themselves should one speak up. And they had.

The horrors had left Hector hollow and very self-aware, despite the foggy dissociation that split him and direct
reality. But on the other hand there was something else here. Something close-by that he could feel, more than see. It
was either doing something important or had done something important. Hector held his hand out, unintuitively trying to
feel the very fabric of time and space.

_Time feels so arbitrary._

Before, or maybe now, he had wandered the city, looking for the gap he should follow. He had wandered through streets,
tunnels, and building. People had asked things of him, tried to rob him, and ultimately detained him. He had been able
to interface with some, but most had resisted him. Despite the setback Hector was still on the path.

He still understood that the only way forwards was to follow the path, but the storm raged on. He had thought that his
time with Del, Andrea, Rikki, and then the rest of them, had come to a close, but somehow they had found him, and their
paths seemed crossed yet again. In fact, Hector now felt like they had led him to success. Deep underground, near the
tunnels of the old city, Hector could sense someone else at work. Some indomitable force of time and space had been
here, and now he was following in its footsteps. Hector knew, knew with all his being, that at the end of this journey,
he would be made whole again. Returned to his former self. Woken from this terrible nightmare that he inhabited beyond
any definable meaning of time. So he moved forwards.

"No way out but through." Hector mumbled to no one in particular.

Ahead of him Hector understood that there was fighting. All around him there was fighting, but ahead of him it was
important. The importance drew him in. Like a moth to the flame, he could not help it.

"Dreamweaver." Hector mumbled again.

The memory had persisted. He remembered the dark tunnels. The hive. They had found it. Waiting. Lurking. Just below the
surface. While Del and Andrea had only seen the few, Hector had seen the many. Countless times. He had watched as it
consumed him, and the other two. Countless deaths, endless suffering, infinite horror. He had learned it could not be
reasoned with. Something had prompted him to find it, and it seemed that the very same thing had provoked it to respond
in unkind fashion. Hector somehow understood it. Dreamweaver was not emotional, just a single emotion. Desperation.
Desperation fulled its mind, and desperation was its only directive. Hector had decided it was best left alone.
Dreamweaver would persist either way.

There was a sudden noise from around a crate in front of Hector, and he glanced upwards from his solemn thoughts. A man
stumbled out from behind a box. He was armed and extremely annoyed. Hector could see his anger radiating from him. It
was a bright flare against the background. Vainly, Hector found himself reaching out in fear. He died.

Hector died right then and there. The man had raised his gun and fired point-blank into hector's chest. Hector did not
feel the bullets, but he felt the loss with himself. Hector knew that under normal circumstances he would have died
right then and there, but for reasons still unclear he did not. Hector finished his step and stood face to face with the
guard. Hector stared deep into his eyes, watching the man's history flash frame in the reflection from his own. Hector
watched the man grow up, struggle with poverty, emotional, and relational hardships. None of it was enough.

Hector blanked out for an indeterminate amount of time, and returned to find the man toppled over behind him, and the
holes in his chest reversing their course. To Hector's surprise another man had appeared in front of him. This second
man had apparently witnessed whatever Hector had done, and was recoiling in fear. Hector heard words bubble out of this
man, but he could absorb their meaning in his head. This new victim's fears washed over and around him, like a boulder
in a river. Hector found himself reaching again for this man. The man stumbled and bumped into a metal shipping
container. More noises came from him. Hector closed the gap now, and touched the man's temple on one side. He made
contact with a single outstretched finger. Hector did not see into this one's past, but instead saw glimpses of either
futures, or perhaps parallels. He had potential. So Hector let him go.

The man stumbled away, holding his head with one hand, gun with the other, and rounded the shipping container. Hector
followed him. Hector followed as the man stumbled out from the cramped rows of plastic boxes and metal containers. Only
two steps behind him, Hector watched as he jerked wildly and collapsed onto the cold concrete floor. Stepping forwards
Hector looked down to ponder what had happened, despite him knowing full well what it was. His dream-self had seen
enough violence and murder to recognize the impact of gunfire stitching up a body. But the wake-self was somewhat
unsure. He glanced down from above the now corpse and looked deep into the dying man's eyes. For some reason this was
different now. Where previously Hector had felt despair and horror at the deaths of those that came before, he now felt
satisfaction. The man had indeed served his purpose, just not what Hector had originally thought. Glancing upwards he
saw Andrea turn away and continue firing bursts into other emerging shapes and figures.

He found he was still sluggish from his earlier death, but he doggedly marched on, clearly seeing the telltale signs
around the door Andrea was making her final stand. AS he got nearer Hector felt his mind begin to waver. His body seemed
to respond fine, but his thoughts were slowly leaking out his brain. It was like someone had flipped an hourglass, and
now Hector's thoughts were slowly siphoning away. Where they went he could not follow, but he found he had to put
increasingly more and more focus just to remain present.

Hector glanced around, unsure what was causing this disturbance, and to his horror, he found the storm had paused. It
was not like before where the elements had simply wavered, like a breeze shifts the rains for a minute, but a full on
break in the clouds. Hector found the break to be the most disconcerting thing he had ever experienced. He was fully
aware of something watching him. Some god was pulling back the clouds to look down on just him. Hector felt heat
traveling up his spine, the opposite of what one might expect when facing down a superior being. He craned his neck, and
dared to look up, not because he knew anything was up there, but out of some learned preference for gods and deities.

To his horror, Hector's instinct had been correct. Above him, embedded in the concrete ceiling a story and a half above
them as a single eye. It was huge and lidless, flatly inset within the concrete, and within it an infinite swirling
vortex of pinprick lights. Hector immediately averted his gaze, his more baseline instincts taking over for his
otherwise dreamy and distanced state. Hector found he had to resist the fear that coursed through his veins. Despite the
slow degradation of his thoughts, his instincts seemed to be surging to forefront of his brain, threatening to take over
and reduce him to a purely hind-brain driven man. Panic was surging through his body, mixing with the heat, threatening
to overwhelm him in a cacophony of stimulation. His old habits taking over, Hector found himself quietly repeating to
himself. The words were a paradoxical combination of self affirmations and emotional flagellations that he had
previously resorted to in his former life. _A prayer to myself._ To his complete and utter astonishment, he found
comforting in the otherwise abusive ritual. Hector found a rhythm to the words, and ultimately landed on a harmonic
balance between positive and negative.

Now he felt real comfort.

Over the slowly rising din of battle, and the cosmic turbulence of direct scrutiny, Hector found that he was being
addressed directly. Andrea had grabbed him and was demanding a code for the door that was between them and escape.
Hector tried to quiet his prayer and bring his renewed surge of self to focus on finding the answer. In the swirling
maelstrom, he witnessed countless attempts, and countless failures. He found he could not help but list one of them out
loud. To his dismay, Andrea tried it and cursed at either him, or their worsening predicament. He reached deep inside
himself. Drawing on his already strained sense of self to fuel his next attempt. Suddenly a surge of heat blossomed from
deep in his chest. One of the wounds was finally stitching itself closed. He read out a different number. Andrea turned
away from him, but he did not need to wait for her to turn back to know the result. Hector strained. He leaned heavily
into the wall, the combined strain of willpower and battle rapidly overtaking his abilities. Andrea yelled something
again. Hector looked into her eyes. For a split second he saw her entirely.

Underneath her hard born exterior he saw the little girl. The tired, the lonely, the angry girl who fought everyone and
everything just to survive. She had learned from experience, and then molded it to her own skills. But underneath the
rage, she was scared. Right now she was scared. Terrified. Death drove her to her limit. She could not reconcile its
finality, its unpreparedness.

Hector felt that too. But he had died many times. In that split second Hector felt his mind tick into place. A set of
gears lining up perfectly, a slot conforming to joint, his brain putting everything together at once. Hector felt a
stream of numbers spill out of his mouth. One after another they bubbled out, and Andrea entered them. He could not
predict this outcome. The perfect crystalline matrices of his brain, perfectly aligned facets, would not let him.
Hector, Andrea, Del, and the lost all hung there in momentary limbo.

**beep**

Above the battle din, imagined or not, dream walking puppet that he was, Hector heard the touchpad chime. Above him,
Hector saw the eye start to spin and melt away. It twisted and warped itself into a smaller and smaller swirl until it
vanished from the cold hard concrete. In its place deep swirls and waves remained embedded in the otherwise featureless
concrete ceiling. Hector felt himself pulled forwards, past heavy steel doors, into a deep and cavernous tunnel.
Shoulders bumped him on both sides as the people who had fought for him, for themselves, struggled past him into the
safety of the dark and damp escape. Hector felt himself jostled back and forth but managed to turn back towards the
cavernous lab. _One last look._ Hector knew he had seen it before. The swirling, the undulations. It was so familiar to
him, but he could not rectify it. Some damaged part of his brain recognized it, but a trauma seemed to obscure the
origin. Hector felt on the verge of remembering, when something interrupted him. The doors were sliding shut, but he was
standing perfectly in the middle. A perfect silhouette against the darkness. There was a quiet zip noise, and then
suddenly Hector felt quite heavy. His body stiffened, and Hector felt the world tilt backwards.

He had died. Again. Infinitely.
