title: in death we march

> UNII Corps log date 2327-09-31-2334 5min earlier

Hector found time to be less linear than usual.

It was the strangest sensation. He felt like the current that had been slowing him down, the weighty drag that
constantly restricted his movements, was not actually restricting him, but rather a restriction by himself. He felt, but
had yet been able to verify, that if he strained hard enough, he would be able to pierce the current that encircled him
and see and move through time. Even more confusingly the restriction 'felt' like it was something he was actively doing.
Hector likened it to being in two places at once, with one version fighting to move, the other pushing back to stop
'himself' from moving too fast. His brain struggled to piece it all together.

On top of it all, the others were constantly interrupting him and prodding him forwards. On one hand he was grateful to
be leaving this place, with its terrible tortured victims. The victims. He routinely found himself a silent and
abhorrent witness to the worst crimes imaginable. People dissected alive, experimented, discarded, and killed. Hopefully
killed. With infinite kaleidoscopic views, Hector had witnessed countless of these atrocities on unending repeat.
Screams of agony, terror, and rage reverberated through his very bones. Humans turned into the medical equivalent of
spare parts, and indeed some were, and left dissembled in vats. Nobody was safe here. Even the experimenters were at
constant risk of becoming experimented themselves should one speak up. And they had.

The horrors had left Hector hollow and very self-aware, despite the foggy dissociation that split him and direct
reality. But on the other hand there was something else here. Something close-by that he could feel, more than see. It
was either doing something important or had done something important. Hector held his hand out, unintuitively trying to
feel the very fabric of time and space.

_Time feels so arbitrary._

Before, or maybe now, he had wandered the city, looking for the gap he should follow. He had wandered through streets,
tunnels, and building. People had asked things of him, tried to rob him, and ultimately detained him. He had been able
to interface with some, but most had resisted him. Despite the setback Hector was still on the path.

He still understood that the only way forwards was to follow the path, but the storm raged on. He had thought that his
time with Del, Andrea, Rikki, and then the rest of them, had come to a close, but somehow they had found him, and their
paths seemed crossed yet again. In fact, Hector now felt like they had led him to success. Deep underground, near the
tunnels of the old city, Hector could sense someone else at work. Some indomitable force of time and space had been
here, and now he was following in its footsteps. Hector knew, knew with all his being, that at the end of this journey,
he would be made whole again. Returned to his former self. Woken from this terrible nightmare that he inhabited beyond
any definable meaning of time. So he moved forwards.

"No way out but through." Hector mumbled to no one in particular.

Ahead of him Hector understood that there was fighting. All around him there was fighting, but ahead of him it was
important. The importance drew him in. Like a moth to the flame, he could not help it.

"Dreamweaver." Hector mumbled again.

The memory had persisted. He remembered the dark tunnels. The hive in which Del, Andrea and him had found it. Waiting.
Lurking just below the surface. Hector had seen it countless times in time. He had watched as it consumed him, and the
other two. It could not be reasoned with, Hector had concluded. Something had prompted him to find it, and it seemed
that the very same thing had provoked it to respond in unkind fashion. Hector somehow understood it. Dreamweaver was not
emotional, just a single emotion. Desperation. Desperation fulled its mind, and desperation was its only directive.
Hector had decided it was best left alone. Dreamweaver would persist.

There was a sudden noise from around a crate in front of Hector, and he glanced upwards from his solemn thoughts. A man
stumbled out from behind a box. He was armed and extremely annoyed. Hector could see his anger radiating from him. It
was a bright flare against the background. Vainly, Hector found himself reaching out in fear. He died.

Hector died right then and there. The man had raised his gun and fired point-blank into hector's chest. Hector did not
feel the bullets, but he felt the loss with himself. Hector knew that under normal circumstances he would have died
right then and there, but for reasons still unclear he did not. Hector finished his step and stood face to face with the
guard. Hector stared deep into his eyes, watching the man's history flash frame in the reflection from his own. Hector
watched the man grow up, struggle with poverty, emotional, and relational hardships. None of it was enough.

Hector blanked out for an indeterminate amount of time, and returned to find the man toppled over behind him, and the
holes in his chest reversing their course. To Hector's surprise another man had appeared in front of him. This second
man had apparently witnessed whatever Hector had done, and was recoiling in fear. Hector heard words bubble out of this
man, but he could absorb their meaning in his head. This new victim's fears washed over and around him, like a boulder
in a river. Hector found himself reaching again for this man. The man stumbled and bumped into a metal shipping
container. More noises came from him. Hector closed the gap now, and touched the man's temple on one side. He made
contact with a single outstretched finger. Hector did not see into this one's past, but instead saw glimpses of either
futures, or perhaps parallels. He had potential. So Hector let him go.

The man stumbled away, holding his head with one hand, gun with the other, and rounded the shipping container. Hector
followed him.
