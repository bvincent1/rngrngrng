> UNII Corps log date 2327-10-01-0004 6.5h later

Del felt the weight with each step. In the cold, dark, and damp tunnels it felt as is the very concrete above them
weighed him down. They had not been walking for long, but after all that had happened, he was unsure how much longer he
could keep going. If he strained, Del felt like he could hear pounding coming from behind them. Deep in the tunnel, he
convinced himself of a rhythmic thumping of cold metal on metal. It hastened his steps, and kept him moving despite the
pain that was welling up inside him.

They trudged onwards, through puddles and darkness. He and the others, all worse for wear, but unable to stop. He could
hear an occasional breath and the patter of their boots in shallow water, but otherwise they were all minding their own.
Everyone was keeping pace. Nobody wanted to give hint of weakness or ask to wait. Nobody wanted to stop out of fear.
Fear of getting caught, fear of what lay behind them, or fear of what lay ahead of them. Their meager lights only
illuminated the barest of trails in the dark. Multiple times during their short escape Del had almost tripped on
upheaved steel track or piles of broken concrete. But together, they kept jogging.

He was almost completely out of breath when they came upon a bend with faint light falling just beyond. O'Rhonn signaled
them all to halt, and Del gingerly set the dead weight down. Andrea watched him closely, clearly worried that Hector was
still unconscious. Del knelt down next to him out of exhaustion, and struggled to summon any willpower. He rolled Hector
over onto his back. Del did his best to inspect him for blood, but he did not see any obvious wounds. The only injury
Del could find was a strange crack in Hector's forehead. He abandoned his search to focus on himself. His shaky hands
fumbled with the rifle strapped to his chest, and he desperately tried to will them into functioning properly. A firm
hand clasped his shoulder, and Del looked up to see Tokolov with a comforting look on his face.

Del had not had much of a chance to express his feelings before, having been expressly wrapped up in the storm of events
that followed, but he felt a sorrow well up deep inside him. He had worked hard to try and bury this sorrow, but having
them back, even if only a few, brought back emotions that Del had no time for here and now. What made it even harder for
Del was seeing the jagged scar that snaked out from under the patch Tokolov wore over his one eye. The injury was new to
Del, but clearly not to Tokolov. The wound was silvery and extended out from cheek to temple, peeking out of both sides
from underneath the makeshift cloth that made up the patch covering his face. The old man's hair was still dark, but Del
thought there was more silver in it than he remembered, bringing Del to wonder if his time since they had last been a
team had been harsheer than his own. His gaze must have lingered a bit too long, as Tokolov screwed up his face in an
awkward sort of embarrassment. The hand released him, and Tokolov strode to the front of their little formation.

Del found he could properly ready his weapon now and he did so as quickly as he could. There was faint mutering coming
from O'Rhonn and the rest, and Del finally stood and made to join them.

"Munrhoe, Tokolov, you two with me. We're gonna check out the light. Sigs, wait here with O'Delt and our guests."
O'Rhonn whispered.

"Wait, I'm good. I can do it." Del gasped. He still out of breath, but his energy was coming back to him.

"It's not up for debate." O'Rhonn whispered harshly at him.

"Hey, give the kid a break. He's still one of us." Tokolov spoke quietly, but with genuine praise on his tone.

"I'm not doing this right now." O'Rhonn said, clearly running out of patience. "My orders stand. Let's go." Without
another word, he spun and slunk away into the darkness.

Del watched as the other two joined O'Rhonn, their forms melting into the shadows on either side of the tunnel. Del felt
his shoulders dip and a sudden wave of exhaustion washed over him.

"What was that about?" He quietly asked, turning to Sigmund.

"Dunno, but let's get you checked over." Sigmund "And your friends." He hastily added.

"I'm good, lets check with her and then him."

Sigmond tapped Del on the arm, right where a hole revealed a filthy patch of bandage underneath.

"Oh, that's from earlier. Let's get them first."

Del turned to see that Andrea was milling around with her gun slung over her shoulder. She was clearly not in hurry one
way or the other. Del felt a gentle prod at his shoulder and found that Sigmund was trying to gesture something to him.
Del had to concentrate to bring it back, but after a moment he recognized their shared language and understood what
Sigmund was trying to ask.

"Hey Andrea, this is Sigmund. He's gonna look you over, make sure you're not too banged up." Del said.

Andrea's head snapped to face them, and Del immediately emotionally braced himself for a stream of angry words to sweep
him away. To his surprise, she merely shrugged. Sigmund took that as acceptance, and moved behind her to begin his
examination. Del decided to follow him, curious to ask her something.

"So what was all that earlier?" Del asked.

"You'll have to be more specific." Andrea said, exasperation in her voice.

"In the lab. You were off." Del clarified.

Del saw Sigmund tap a finger on her shoulder, and she turned her head to face him. He raised his hands defensively.
"Your plate took a hit. Need to double-check." Sigmund said hastily.

Andrea fixed Sigmund with a cold glare, and then turned to Del to fix him with an equally icy look. After a few seconds
trying to freeze him in place, she grunted and lifted her arms to release buckles holder her carrier. Sigmund helped and
within a few seconds the two had the vest loose enough for Sigmund to continue. Del saw a pained look on her face, and
noticed a flurry of movement from Sigmund.

"O'Delt, get over here and hold this." Sigmund hastily ordered.

Del hurried over and gingerly reached his hand underneath the vest and onto her abdomen. He was only a few inches
underneath, but he felt a flush of embarrassment. Despite the cold, he still felt a warm dampness on his fingers.
Andrea's face was screwed up in pain, but she didn't make any noise. Sigmund was hurriedly digging through his bag at
their feet. Del looked back towards Andrea and suddenly felt too close. Sigmund stood back up, bandage in hand, and
lifter the front of the vest up to get a better view. Del did his best to step out of the way, while simultaneously
taking the weight of the vest with his free hand.

"Ok, O'Delt, you can let go. It's not bad, just a small puncture." Sigmund said, his voice calm and even. "Get her vest
off though, I'm gonna patch it up."

Del pulled his hand back and saw that it was not covered in blood, but merely lightly stained. He released a breath he
had not noticed he was holding, and he suddenly became aware of his pounding heart. Andrea squirmed slightly and Del
broke away from examining his hand to finally lift the plate carrier off her shoulders and over her head. After a second
of struggling, he finally lifted it high enough for her hair to fall away and land on her shoulders. Del was struck by
her eyes, which were fixed on him. She was clearly in pain, but something about her look told him that she was deep in
contemplation. After a moment, she spoke.

"I'm coming off hypers." She said, her tone neutral and even. "It's been too long since I re-upped, and now I'm going
through withdrawal."

She winced slightly as Sigmund applied something to her abdomen. Del had heard faint cutting noises a moment ago and
assumed that Sigmund was cleaning the wound. Del did his best to keep focused on Andrea's eyes, but the prolonged eye
contact was making him uncomfortable. She did not seem to share his discomfort.

"Probably speed-ran the symptoms." Del quipped.

"I still feel like shit. Just not actively shit." Andrea continued in her even tone and eye contact. "I can still keep
up with you boys."

Del realized that this tone of hers was really just a face she was putting on to keep up appearances. She was clearly in
pain, but refused to show it in front of all these strangers. _I'm probably not helping like this._

Sigmund quietly took her hand and directed her to apply pressure herself on the bandage he had just applied. They both
looked down and saw that Sigmund had almost finished his patch job, and was finishing up with some tape. Andrea gingerly
did as she was told, and a few moments later the entire operation was done. Del stood there, involuntarily drawn to
watch as she inspected the work and fretted over the hole Sigmund had made in her jacket.

"Could've warned me 'Sigs'." She growled at him.

Sigmund paused midway through packing up his bag, clearly deciding how best to respond.

"Hole was already there, I just made it bigger." He said, doing his best attempt to keep a steady professionalism in his
voice. "We'll have to get you a new carrier though, your old one in dented." He nodded towards Del, and the vest that he
had been absentmindedly holding.

Del turned and lifted the vest so that everyone could see it in the limited light. Indeed, Sigmund had been correct,
there was a crack on the front and a jagged piece of metal was rattling around somewhere in the fabric on the inner side
of the bulletproofing garb. He let the vest down, and turned it to Andrea.

"Need anything from this?" Del asked, indicating the mags strapped to the front.

"Not particularly." Andrea said, hefting her rifle back onto her good side.

She eyed'd him expectantly. "But you've got something else of mine."

Del Paused for a moment, unsure what she was referring to, and then remembered. He unceremoniously dropped the carrier
on the ground and then reached behind his back to grasp the handle of her pistol.

"Forgot." Del said nonchalantly.

"I'll bet." Andrea said, her tone dripping with sarcasm. "In fact, you all seem to be pretty good at this. Acquiring
stuff." She took a step back and turned to include Sigmund in the conversation. "How is it that you even have medical
equipment, weapons, armor? Where'd you even get this stuff? What, did they leave it with in when they tossed in those
cells?"

Sigmund finished organizing his bag, and zipped it shut. He did not make eye contact with her, he just spoke into the
bag.

"Found it all in the crates back under that lab. Lot's of stuff there. Pretty sure O'Rhonn has a couple grenades.
Might've gotten those from a guard."

"And you?" Andrea directed this particular question at Del. "Where did you get all this?" She gestured to him and his
kit.

Del reflexively shifted his weight did a mental assessment of what he had on his person.

"Same." he responded.

Del watched as Andrea's face subtly shifted through a range of emotions. She was doing her best to contain herself, but
she had clearly been through a lot and was already being dragged down by her withdrawal. She eventually declined to ask
more, and resigned herself to walking away.

"What about him?" Sigmund said, nodding to Hector.

Del looked past them both towards where he had put Hector down. To his surprise Hector had stood up. Sigmund went to
move towards Hector, but Del held out his arm, stopping him.

"I think I'll check him." Del said, not feeling particularly sure of himself.

Sigmund merely nodded and went towards the corner, watching for the return of the others. Del carefully approached
Hector, who was upright and staring directly towards him. His eyes were dim in the poor lighting, but Del saw that they
were glowing ever so faintly. Hector was statuesque in his body language, but Del saw a small crack in his forehead. It
might have been the light, but Del could almost make out ever shrinking sparks coming from the injury.

"You ok? Where'd you get hit?" Del asked Hector cautiously.

There was a pause before Hector spoke. "Yes. Close."

"What?" Del asked.

"He's close." Hector repeated.

"Who? Alphason?" Del asked, suddenly very focused on Hector's words instead of his injuries.

"Make sure to mention the gap." Hector mumbled.

"What? What gap?" Del asked, confused.

"What'd he say?" Andrea asked from behind him.

"He said he's close. And something about a gap?" Del said over his shoulder, making sure to keep eye contact with
Hector.

"He's making less and less sense." Andrea said, motioning towards Hector. "I think whatever happens to him is slowly
taking over."

"What happens to him?" Del asked, unsure what she meant.

Andrea fixed him with an icy stare. "When he dies, and comes back."

"What do you mean?" Del said, incredulous.

"Hector is immortal. Or something like it." Andrea said mater-of-factly. "I've seen him take 3 to the chest, and even a
slug to the face only to just walk it off. I think this time he took a round to the head, but it's getting slower. He's
getting slower."

Del just stood there, unsure how to process this. He had always just assumed that Hector had not been hit this whole
time. Or if he had, it had been minor. Andrea's words shook him. Even more so, she seemed to think he was getting worse.

"And you're just ok with that? That he's immortal?" Del asked.

"Well I mean..." she trailed off. "What am I supposed to do about it? Alphason is clearly on the same stuff, or whatever
it is. Look at what he's done, and then look at Hector here. There both changing. It's not up to us."

"What makes you think it's both of them?"

"I mean look around Del. Alphason has clearly been here." Andrea said, gesturing to the tunnel walls on either side of
them.

Del stood there, giving her his best look of complete doubt, then took a moment to process whether she was being
philosophical or not. She redoubled her gesture, insistingly towards the tunnel walls, the concrete. He walked towards
the wall, unsure what he was supposed to be looking for.
